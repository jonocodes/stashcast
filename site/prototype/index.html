Local Listener Refuses to Subscribe to Entire Podcast for One Episode

Builds tool instead. Any link — video, lecture, single episode — now lands in his podcast app, ready offline.

By our self-hosting correspondent · Filed from a home server in a closet

It started, as these things do, with a text message. “You have to hear this episode,” a friend wrote, attaching a link to a show the recipient had never heard of and had no intention of following.

The suspect's logo, photographed shortly after stashing a two-hour lecture.

Rather than subscribe, he built StashCast: a small, self-hosted app that downloads whatever is at the link and publishes it as a private podcast feed.

“I don't want the whole show,” he told reporters. “I want that one episode. Later. On my walk. At 1.5×.”

The tool accepts anything the popular yt-dlp downloader understands, plus direct media files and ordinary web pages with players embedded in them.

A bookmarklet sends the current page with a single click. A background worker does the rest: fetching metadata, artwork and subtitles, and, if asked, a short summary for the show notes.

Moments later the episode appears in AntennaPod, Overcast or any other app that reads RSS. Neighbours describe the setup as “one compose file.”
